Read the GPA column carefully

These figures are not comparable to each other, and sorting the column will mislead you if you forget that. Every badge matters: a W at 4.47 (UNC) and a ? at 3.90 (Vanderbilt) are measuring different things. Anything above 4.00 is necessarily weighted — that’s arithmetic, not an assumption. Below 4.00, most schools simply never say, so the badge reads ? rather than guessing.

Why 62 schools are blank

The Common Data Set asks for average high school GPA but doesn’t require an answer, and the most selective privates routinely leave it empty — Cornell, Northwestern, Brown, Columbia, Notre Dame, Georgetown, Tufts, Texas A&M, UT Austin and Ohio State all publish nothing. UVA says so outright: no GPA targets or cutoffs. Blank means the school withheld it, not that it’s low.

Out-of-state is sometimes the easier door

The gap runs negative more often than you’d expect. Binghamton admits 29% of New Yorkers and 74% of everyone else — a 45-point swing in your daughter’s favor. Same direction at Stony Brook, Minnesota, UConn, UMass Amherst, and most of the UCs, which court non-residents for the tuition. Sort the gap column ascending to find them.

Four schools use a 100-point scale

Stony Brook (94.5), Buffalo (91.6), RIT (94.0) and Albany (88–96) report a New York high-school average rather than a 4.0 GPA. Rather than convert them with a made-up formula, they’re left blank with a note on the row.

Residency gap is the column to read

Both rates show at once for 90 publics, computed from the residency table in each school’s own Common Data Set. Sort by the gap: UT Austin admits 36% of Texans and 8% of everyone else. UNC 37% vs. 10%. Georgia Tech 29% vs. 10%. From New Jersey, only Rutgers is an in-state row — every other number in this table is the out-of-state one.

Where the rest comes from

Enrollment, SAT percentiles, overall admit rate, tuition and cost of attendance are from the U.S. Department of Education College Scorecard (June 2026 release). Ranks are U.S. News 2026 Best National Universities. GPA and residency splits were read one school at a time out of 190 Common Data Sets and first-year class profiles.

Two things that will shift

U.S. News publishes its 2027 edition on Sept. 22, 2026, adding a graduate-earnings metric they expect to move schools more than usual. And cost here is sticker price — tuition, fees, room, board, supplies — not what most families pay after aid. Out-of-state totals swap in the non-resident tuition line and hold room and board constant.
